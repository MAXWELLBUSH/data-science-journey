Data & Data Analytics — 30 Questions and Answers
1. Fundamentals of Data
1. What is data?

Answer:
Data is a collection of raw facts, observations, or values that can be stored and processed by a computer. Data may include numbers, text, images, audio, video, and other forms of information.

2. What is the difference between data and information?

Answer:
Data is raw and unprocessed facts, while information is data that has been processed, organized, and given context so that it becomes meaningful and useful.

Example:

Data:        10, 20, 30, 40
Information: Sales increased steadily over the period.
3. What is the DIKW Pyramid?

Answer:
The DIKW Pyramid describes how raw data can be transformed into increasingly meaningful forms:

Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom

Data: Raw facts
Information: Processed data with meaning
Knowledge: Understanding gained from information
Wisdom: Applying knowledge and judgment to make decisions
4. What is a data table?

Answer:
A data table is a structured arrangement of data in rows and columns, where each column usually represents a variable and each row represents an observation or record.

Example:

Name	Age	Salary
John	25	50,000
Mary	30	60,000
5. How are different types of data represented inside a computer?

Answer:
Computers ultimately represent data using binary digits (0 and 1). However, different types of data use different encoding and representation methods. For example, text can use Unicode/UTF-8, while images consist of numerical information representing pixels.

2. Big Data
6. What is Big Data?

Answer:
Big Data refers to extremely large, complex, and rapidly generated datasets that can be difficult to store, process, and analyze using traditional data-processing tools.

7. What are the 5 Vs of Big Data?

Answer:

Volume — the huge amount of data.
Velocity — the speed at which data is generated and processed.
Variety — the different forms of data.
Veracity — the reliability and quality of the data.
Value — the useful insights that can be extracted from the data.
8. Give an example of Big Data.

Answer:
A social media platform can generate huge amounts of:

Posts
Comments
Likes
Images
Videos
Messages
Location data
Search activity

The enormous volume, variety, and velocity of this information make it an example of Big Data.

9. Why is Big Data important?

Answer:
Big Data allows organizations to discover patterns, understand customers, improve operations, make better decisions, predict future outcomes, and develop new products and services.

3. Data Science, Business & Systems Theory
10. What is Data Science?

Answer:
Data Science is an interdisciplinary field that uses statistics, mathematics, programming, algorithms, and domain knowledge to extract insights and build solutions from data.

11. What three major areas intersect in Data Science?

Answer:

Computer Science / IT
Mathematics & Statistics
Domain / Business Knowledge

A data scientist needs technical skills as well as an understanding of the problem or industry they are working in.

12. What is the main difference between Data Analytics and Data Science?

Answer:
Data Analytics primarily focuses on understanding data and discovering insights, while Data Science generally has a broader scope that includes predictive modeling, machine learning, and deployment of data-driven solutions.

For example:

Data Analyst:

Why did sales decrease?

Data Scientist:

Can we predict which customers are likely to stop buying?

13. What is Systems Theory?

Answer:
Systems Theory studies systems as a whole and focuses on how their individual components interact and influence one another.

A system consists of interconnected components working toward a goal.

14. What are the four basic components of a system?

Answer:

Inputs — resources or information entering the system.
Processes — activities that transform the inputs.
Outputs — results produced by the system.
Feedback — information from the output that can influence future processes or inputs.
Inputs → Processes → Outputs
   ↑                   ↓
   └────── Feedback ────┘
4. Approaches in Data Analysis
15. What are the main steps in the Data Analysis process?

Answer:

Problem Statement
Data Collection
Data Cleaning
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Gathering and Reporting Insights
16. What is a problem statement?

Answer:
A problem statement clearly defines the problem, question, or gap that the analysis is intended to investigate.

A good problem statement should be:

Specific
Clear
Concise
Unbiased
Measurable
17. What is data cleaning?

Answer:
Data cleaning is the process of identifying and correcting problems in raw data, such as:

Missing values
Duplicate records
Incorrect formats
Inconsistent values
Irrelevant observations
Errors
18. What is Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)?

Answer:
EDA is the process of examining and summarizing a dataset to understand its distributions, patterns, relationships, trends, and outliers using statistics and visualizations.

19. What is the difference between univariate and multivariate analysis?

Answer:

Univariate analysis examines one variable at a time.

Example:

What is the distribution of customer ages?

Multivariate analysis examines relationships between two or more variables.

Example:

Is there a relationship between advertising expenditure and sales?

20. What are the four levels of analytics?

Answer:

Descriptive — What happened?
Diagnostic — Why did it happen?
Predictive — What will happen?
Prescriptive — What should we do?

A simple way to remember them:

What → Why → What next → What should we do?

21. What is Descriptive Analytics?

Answer:
Descriptive Analytics summarizes historical data to determine what happened.

Example:

Sales decreased by 15% in July.

Common tools include reports, dashboards, and charts.

22. What is Diagnostic Analytics?

Answer:
Diagnostic Analytics investigates why something happened.

Example:

Sales decreased because inventory shortages caused products to become unavailable.

Techniques include drill-down analysis, data mining, and root-cause analysis.

23. What is Predictive Analytics?

Answer:
Predictive Analytics uses historical data, statistics, and machine learning to estimate what is likely to happen in the future.

Example:

Next month's sales are predicted to increase by 10%.

24. What is Prescriptive Analytics?

Answer:
Prescriptive Analytics determines what action should be taken by using techniques such as optimization, simulation, and decision models.

Example:

Increase inventory by 15% to reduce the probability of stockouts.

5. The World of Data Practitioners
25. Who is a data practitioner?

Answer:
A data practitioner is a professional who works with data to collect, manage, analyze, interpret, visualize, model, or communicate data-driven insights.

Examples include:

Data Analysts
Data Scientists
Data Engineers
Business Intelligence Analysts
Machine Learning Engineers
26. What does a Data Analyst typically do?

Answer:
A Data Analyst typically:

Collects data
Cleans data
Analyzes data
Creates visualizations
Builds dashboards
Identifies trends and patterns
Communicates insights to stakeholders

Common tools include SQL, Excel, Power BI, Tableau, and Python.

27. What does a Data Scientist typically do?

Answer:
A Data Scientist uses statistics, programming, and machine learning to:

Analyze data
Build predictive models
Develop machine learning solutions
Evaluate models
Extract complex insights
Sometimes deploy models into production

For example, building a movie recommendation model is a typical Data Science task.

28. What does a Data Engineer do?

Answer:
A Data Engineer builds and maintains the infrastructure required to collect, store, transform, and make data available for analysts and data scientists.

For example:

Data Sources
     ↓
Data Pipelines
     ↓
Data Storage
     ↓
Data Warehouse
     ↓
Analysts / Data Scientists
6. The Data Practitioner's Guide
29. Why is business/domain knowledge important for a data practitioner?

Answer:
Technical skills alone aren't enough. A practitioner needs to understand the business problem, industry context, objectives, and stakeholders so they can interpret the data correctly and produce useful solutions.

For example, a data scientist may build an accurate sales prediction model, but without understanding the business, they may not know how the prediction should actually be used.

30. What is the ultimate goal of working with data?

Answer:
The ultimate goal is not simply to collect or analyze data. It is to turn data into useful insights and actions that create value and solve real-world problems.

The overall process can be summarized as:
