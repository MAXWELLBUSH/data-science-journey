Fundamentals of Data Science
1. Fundamentals of Data Science
What is Data Science?

Definition:
Data science is an interdisciplinary field that uses scientific methods, processes, algorithms, and systems to extract knowledge and actionable insights from both structured and unstructured data.

Core Goal:
The goal of data science is to transform raw data into useful insights that can solve real-world problems and improve decision-making.

The Intersection of Fields

Data science sits at the intersection of three major areas:

Field	Contribution to Data Science
Computer Science / IT	Programming, data engineering, databases, software, and system architecture
Mathematics & Statistics	Statistical analysis, probability, predictive modeling, and machine learning algorithms
Domain / Business Knowledge	Understanding the industry, identifying problems, interpreting results, and making decisions

A successful data scientist needs to understand all three areas.

2. Business Theory & Organization
What is a Business?

A business is an organization or entity involved in commercial, industrial, or professional activities.

Primary Objective

A business generally aims to:

Deliver value to customers
Generate revenue and profit
Use resources efficiently
Achieve its organizational goals

Not every organization is profit-oriented, but every organization generally has objectives it seeks to achieve.

Core Functional Areas

Businesses have different departments that work together and generate and use data.

Business Function	What It Does	Example Data
Marketing & Sales	Attracting customers and generating revenue	Customer behavior, sales, campaign performance
Operations & Supply Chain	Managing production, logistics, inventory, and resources	Inventory levels, delivery times, production data
Finance & Accounting	Managing money and financial activities	Revenue, expenses, budgets, cash flow
Human Resources (HR)	Managing employees and talent	Employee performance, hiring, retention
Why Does This Matter to Data Science?

A data scientist needs to understand where the data comes from and what the business is trying to achieve.

For example:

Sales Department
       ↓
Generates sales data
       ↓
Data Scientist analyzes it
       ↓
Finds declining sales
       ↓
Identifies possible causes
       ↓
Business takes action

So data science isn't just about analyzing numbers. You need to understand the business problem behind the numbers.

3. Systems Theory & Systems Thinking
What is Systems Theory?

Systems theory is the study of systems and how their different components interact with one another.

A system consists of interconnected components that work together toward a particular goal.

For example, a business can be viewed as a system:

                 BUSINESS SYSTEM


Inputs ───────► Processes ───────► Outputs
  ▲                                  │
  │                                  │
  └──────────── Feedback ◄───────────┘
Components of a System

1. Inputs

Resources or information entering the system.

Examples:

Raw data
Money
Employees
Materials
Customer orders

2. Processes

Activities that transform inputs into something useful.

Examples:

Manufacturing
Data processing
Calculations
Business operations
Machine learning algorithms

3. Outputs

The results produced by the system.

Examples:

Products
Services
Reports
Predictions
Business decisions

4. Feedback

Information about the output that is used to improve future inputs or processes.

Example: Online Store

Imagine an online shopping system.

Customer Order
      ↓
     INPUT
      ↓
Order Processing
      ↓
    PROCESS
      ↓
Product Delivered
      ↓
    OUTPUT
      ↓
Customer Feedback
      ↓
   FEEDBACK
      ↓
Improve Service

The system continuously learns from its outputs and feedback.

4. Bridging Data Science, Business, and Systems

Data science becomes especially powerful when we combine data science + business knowledge + systems thinking.

Systemic Business Perspective

Instead of looking at one isolated problem, a data scientist considers how different parts of the business interact.

For example, a decline in sales might not simply be a sales problem.

It could be caused by:

Supply problems
      ↓
Products unavailable
      ↓
Fewer sales
      ↓
Lower revenue

Or:

Poor marketing
      ↓
Fewer customers
      ↓
Fewer orders
      ↓
Lower sales
Holistic Solutions

A data scientist should therefore try to understand the whole system, rather than fixing only one isolated component.

Value Creation

When analytics is integrated into business systems, organizations can:

Automate processes
Detect problems earlier
Make better decisions
Predict future outcomes
Continuously improve operations
Increase efficiency
Create better customer experiences
